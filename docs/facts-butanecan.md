# facts-butanecan — แก๊สกระป๋องพกพา (ยังไงนะ)

Sources (S#): S1 Justia patent 20150114493, safety valve for portable gas container (https://patents.justia.com/patent/20150114493) · S2 Iwatani FORE WINDS FW-LS01 user manual (https://manuals.online/iwatani/camping-equipment/fore-winds-fw-ls01/user-manual/17bi5) · S3 Hunting Waterfalls, "Do butane canisters expire" (https://huntingwaterfalls.com/do-butane-canisters-expire/) · S4 general physics (vapour pressure rises with temperature; evaporative cooling), no single source

| # | Claim in script | Source(s) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Canister holds fuel (mostly butane) liquefied under pressure; vapour sits above the liquid | S4, S1 (pressurised container) | High (composition varies: butane/isobutane/propane blends; script says "ส่วนใหญ่เป็นบิวเทน") |
| 2 | A spring-loaded valve with a rubber seal keeps the canister closed until the stem is pressed; the stove pin presses the stem and opens it | S1 (spring + rubber gasket valve in the mounting cup), general design | Medium-High (S1 describes a pressure-relief valve; the stove-side opening pin is general design knowledge, schematic) |
| 3 | As vapour is drawn off, liquid boils to replace it and the can cools; low pressure then weakens the flame | S4 | High (physics), no product source |
| 4 | Vapour pressure rises with temperature; a large pan reflecting heat onto the can is a typical overheating cause | S2 (warns about oversized pots, missing drip pan/grate causing overheating), S1 (pressure rise when heated) | High |
| 5 | Some stoves have a pressure-sensor safety device: when canister pressure/temperature gets abnormally high the canister is pushed off the stove and the flame goes out | S2 (Iwatani: "canister detaches automatically and the flame goes out") | High for that maker; script says "เตาบางรุ่น" |
| 6 | Once detached, the spring-loaded valve closes again | S1, general design | Medium-High (inference) |
| 7 | Some canisters have a pressure-release vent for extreme heat to lower rupture risk | S3 (CRV feature), S1 (relief valve concept) | Medium (secondary source). The animation draws the vent at the can base; the exact location/design differs by brand and is schematic, the script does not state a location |
| 8 | Safety devices are the last line of defence; manufacturer manual: right-size pots (S2: under about 9.5 in / 24 cm for that stove), never use without drip pan/grate, not in enclosed spaces (CO), use only the specified canister | S2 | High |
| 9 | Nudge: manufacture date is usually stamped on the base; check for rust, dents, valve/seal wear | S3 | Medium (single secondary source; S3 also claims 8-10 year shelf life, NOT used) |

Animation notes: canister is drawn upright and the stove block is schematic; real cassette stoves mount the can sideways with a magnet/lever. Valve spring, gauge and molecule counts are illustrative. The pressure bar is not to scale and shows no numbers.

Safety wording: script never gives instructions for defeating or testing safety devices; it only repeats manufacturer cautions. Temperature thresholds (e.g. S2 about 40 C trigger, S3 50 C storage limit) intentionally NOT stated.

Not used because unverified: exact trigger pressure/temperature of cut-off devices, CRV expansion/location, shelf-life years, Thai standard (มอก.) references, what to do with a leaking canister beyond "check".
