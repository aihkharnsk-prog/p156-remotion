| 1 | Green LED(s) + silicon photodiode sit on the back of the watch; reflective mode (LED and photodiode in the same plane, light scatters back through tissue to the detector) | S1, S2 | High |
| 2 | Blood absorbs strongly in the green (peaks near 540 and 570 nm); commercial sensors mostly use ~530 nm "true green" LEDs. Script says only "แสงสีเขียว" | S1, S2 | High |
| 3 | Absorber is haemoglobin in red blood cells (S1/S2 say "blood"; haemoglobin is standard physiology) | S1, S2 (blood), general physiology | Medium-High |
| 4 | Each heartbeat changes arterial blood volume, which changes absorption and so the detected light (AC part of the signal, perfusion index = AC/DC) | S1, S2 | High |
| 5 | Signal is band-pass filtered (about 0.4-4 Hz), spikes removed, peaks detected; interval between peaks gives BPM (BPM = 60 / interval in s; the 60/IBI step is standard arithmetic, not from S3) | S3 | High (filtering, peaks) / standard maths (BPM) |
| 6 | Motion artefacts are the largest error source; loose fit lets ambient light / crosstalk in, so tight skin contact and light barriers are designed in | S1, S2, S3 | High |
| 7 | Tattoo ink is opaque; dark inks (black, red) absorb green light so less light returns | S4 (secondary news source, cites CNN) | Medium |
| 8 | Cold narrows blood vessels in the extremities, lowering blood flow and signal strength | S5 | Medium (single non-academic source) |
| 9 | Consumer wellness wearables are positioned for wellness, not to diagnose / treat disease; script wording: "ใช้ดูแนวโน้ม ไม่ได้มีไว้วินิจฉัยโรค", NOT "all watches are not medical devices" (some watches have separately cleared features) | S6 | Medium |
| 10 | Step counting: accelerometer magnitude, smoothing, peak detection with threshold, each max/min pair counted as a step; wrist signal is damped and arm movements can look like steps | S7 | High |
