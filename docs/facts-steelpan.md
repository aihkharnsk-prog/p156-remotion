# facts-steelpan — กระทะสแตนเลส (ยังไงนะ)

Facts only. No brand names in the script. Temperatures and "pores shrink" claims are weak in the sources and are handled conservatively (see Not used).

Sources (S#): S1 A Dente newsletter "Why food sticks to stainless steel and how to stop it" (https://aldente.substack.com/p/why-food-sticks-to-stainless-steel) · S2 PopSci "Use the Leidenfrost effect to make your stainless steel pan non-stick" (https://www.popsci.com/diy/make-stainless-steel-non-stick/) · S3 CulinaryLore "Leidenfrost egg myth" (https://culinarylore.com/food-science:leidenfrost-egg-myth/, opinion piece, asserts more than it cites)

| # | Claim in script | Source(s) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stainless looks smooth but is microscopically rough (peaks and valleys) | S1, S2 | Medium-High |
| 2 | Proteins at the food surface denature with heat and bond to the pan surface, so food sticks; script says "โปรตีนจับตัวเมื่อโดนความร้อน แล้วยึดกับโลหะตามร่อง" | S1 (S2 says no scientific consensus on the exact mechanism) | Medium (mechanism simplified; animation shows strands hooking into valleys, schematic) |
| 3 | Preheat the empty pan before oil; hot oil is less viscous and spreads into the peaks and valleys more easily | S1 | Medium-High |
| 4 | Water-drop test: droplets that bead and glide on a vapor layer mean the pan is hot enough; droplets that just sizzle away mean not yet | S1, S2 | High |
| 5 | Leidenfrost effect: a vapor layer under the droplet keeps it from touching the metal | S2, S3, general physics | High |
| 6 | For stainless the Leidenfrost point is "typically" about 198-215 C; script says only "ราวสองร้อยองศา" | S3 | Medium (single opinion-type source, varies by pan) |
| 7 | After the water test, pour out the water, add oil, then food (oil film between food and metal) | S1, S2 | Medium-High |
| 8 | Moisture from room-temperature food turns to steam and insulates the surface, helping release; script says "ช่วยให้ไม่แนบติดผิว" | S2 | Medium |
| 9 | A crust forms, and once seared the food releases by itself; if it still sticks, do not force the flip | S1, S2 | High |
| 10 | Cold meat straight from the fridge lowers the pan surface temperature and food bonds again | S2 | Medium |
| 11 | Good pans are commonly three-layer: stainless / aluminum / stainless (aluminum conducts heat better and spreads it); script says "หลายรุ่น" | S1 | Medium-High |
| 12 | Stainless is not truly nonstick; it releases because of correct heat plus oil | S2 | High |
| 13 | Nudge (no source): burnt residue or a roughened/scratched surface makes food stick more easily (more/rougher valleys, residue gives something to grab); presented as an observation tip | consistent with S1 mechanism | Medium (reasoning, not a quoted source) |

Animation notes: micro-surface profile, strand shapes, oil film thickness and the bead are schematic and not to scale. No numeric zoom factor is shown.

Not used because unverified: S2's claim that heating makes metal expand and "shrinks the pores"; whether the water-bead/vapor layer helps eggs (S3 argues it does not and that the oil does the work, without cited measurements; the script frames the bead only as a temperature test); exact preheat times; any specific brand's layer thickness.
